# GenomeX vs CheckM2

CheckM2 estimates completeness and contamination with a machine-learning model over a reference proteome database. GenomeX uses BUSCO odb10 single-copy markers plus tetranucleotide composition. The two are not expected to produce identical numbers, and identical numbers would be suspicious. This page separates what must agree from what need not.

72 genomes compared. Generated by `bench/compare_to_checkm2.py`.

**Summary: the deterministic statistics and completeness pass; the contamination
verdict fails.** GenomeX flags 62 of 72 published assemblies while CheckM2 puts
only 4 above 5% contamination, and the median CheckM2 contamination is flat
across GenomeX's three verdict levels. The verdict, as thresholded here, carries
no information. This page is kept as the record of that result.

## Reading this honestly

Three different claims are on this page and they deserve different confidence.

**The deterministic quantities are verified.** Six values computed from the same
FASTA by two independent implementations agree to within rounding across all 72
genomes. Nothing about method or calibration enters; this is as close to proof
as the repository gets.

**Completeness is corroborated.** 0.55 points mean absolute difference against a
neural-network model trained on a reference proteome database, from a marker
scan that shares none of its machinery, over 72 genomes. Two unrelated methods
agreeing that closely is meaningful evidence. The single genome differing by
18.55 points is worth investigating individually rather than averaging away.

**Contamination is refuted.** Not "weakly supported" -- refuted. A verdict whose
median CheckM2 contamination is 1.12% when it says clean and 0.82% when it says
likely is not a weak signal, it is the absence of one, faintly inverted. Recall
of 1.00 is not a defence: flagging 86% of everything catches all positives by
construction.

The cause is identified: the bimodality rule fires on 57 of 72 genomes, 0 of 9
finished assemblies and 57 of 63 drafts, because a 2-means split always returns
two bins and nothing tests whether the split beats chance. The genomic-island
claim in `compare.py` was given a permutation null for exactly this reason; the
same discipline was not applied here.

One thing this page must not become is a target to tune against. Fitting the
contamination thresholds until this confusion matrix improves would be training
on the test set, and the resulting number would mean nothing on any other
collection.
